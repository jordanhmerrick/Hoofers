How it plays

  1. 1

    Pitch · 44 yd

    Pull back, let go. The ball carries out to the middle of the fairway and your golfer sets off after it. You walk 19 feet. That's the kitchen and back.

  2. 2

    Chip · 20 yd

    He's waiting at the ball when you get there. Second swing puts you on the green, past the bunker. You walk 9 feet.

  3. 3

    Putt · 4 yd

    Short pull, gentle roll, and it drops. You walk 2 feet — barely a step, and that's the joke.

  4. ★

    Paid

    Trophy pops. The cow ambles in and looks at you. Hole 2 unlocks, and it's about 90 feet of walking.

Every one of these is a reason to put the phone down before the first walk happens — and the first walk is the only thing that matters. The shop stays locked until hole 3.

The call I'd defend

The first walk should be almost insultingly short. Nineteen feet is a gag — you stand up, take six steps, and the game pays you like you did something.

That gag is what teaches the loop without a tutorial, and it's what makes somebody willing to try a 200-foot walk on hole 4.

The test: an eleven-year-old finishes hole 1 with nobody explaining anything. If she needs a word of help, the hole is wrong, not the player. Nothing here is locked — the yardages, the par and the 1:7 ratio are a first pass, sized so the whole hole fits inside one room.
